Create behavioral interview dataset entries for the "ambiguity" category.

The goal is to generate realistic ambiguity-related interview responses for the PlacePrep behavioral evaluation dataset.

Use this exact JSON schema for every entry:
{
  "id": integer,
  "question": string,
  "category": "ambiguity",
  "answer": string,
  "label": "weak" | "average" | "strong",
  "score_clarity": integer,
  "score_structure": integer,
  "score_impact": integer,
  "missing": [string, ...]
}

Question style:
- Use realistic prompts such as:
  - "Tell me about a time you worked through ambiguity."
  - "Describe a situation where the requirements were unclear."
  - "Tell me about a time you had to move forward without full information."

Category-specific realism:
- Focus on unclear requirements, moving with partial information, asking clarifying questions, testing assumptions, and reducing uncertainty.
- Strong answers should show structured thinking, decision-making, communication, and outcome.
- Weak answers may sound passive, confused, or too vague about what was done.
- Average answers may show effort but limited strategy or impact.

Realism rules:
- Include weak, average, and strong answers.
- Include short, medium, and long answers.
- Some answers should feel natural and a little rough.
- Do not make every answer polished.
- Keep the situations realistic for projects, internships, hackathons, and junior roles.

Scoring guidance:
- weak:
  - clarity 2-4
  - structure 2-4
  - impact 1-3
- average:
  - clarity 5-7
  - structure 4-6
  - impact 4-6
- strong:
  - clarity 7-10
  - structure 7-10
  - impact 7-10

Missing-field guidance:
- Use realistic missing items such as:
  - "decision rationale"
  - "clarifying actions"
  - "specific actions"
  - "result"
  - "stakeholder communication"
  - "reflection"

Formatting rules:
- Output valid JSON only.
- Keep category exactly "ambiguity".
- Ensure every record includes all fields.
- Ensure "missing" is always an array.
- Do not include explanations outside the JSON.
